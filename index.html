Which way does it flow, and does it flow back?

That one question runs through 47 chapters. It is asked of slavery and the Underground Railroad, of the land under a meetinghouse in Dallas County, Iowa, of Indian boarding schools, of Quaker giving, of the draft, and of the economy young people are entering now.

Three documents, arranged as one

The wording is each document's own. A line under each heading says which one it comes from.

Three Concerns About Friends

Poverty, freedom and violence. An answer for young adult Friends and others, with Woolman passages and six group sessions.

Refusing the One-Way Channel

The New Conscientious Objector reread through reciprocity and extraction, from a 1972 letter to a draft board to the present, and why so much Quaker aid took the shape of charity.

Which Way It Flows

Quaker history and a just future for all, written for young adults.

Five questions to ask of anything

A job, a loan, a meetinghouse, a story you tell. No one has to agree about economics first.

  • DirectionWhich way do things flow?
  • ReturnDoes anything flow back, and is it something the other party actually wants?
  • DurationDoes the relationship continue after the need is met, or end there?
  • InvitationWho opened the door? Was the channel entered by invitation, or by assumption?
  • ProtocolWhose rules govern the exchange, and who names the need?
